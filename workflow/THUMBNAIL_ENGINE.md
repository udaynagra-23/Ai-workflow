# Long-Form YouTube Thumbnail Engine

**Purpose:** Create honest, instantly understandable 16:9 thumbnails that make the video's central promise easier to notice and more compelling. This is a reusable production standard, not a guarantee of clicks.

## 1. Selected house formula: Focus + Contrast + Proof + Curiosity

Build the default thumbnail from four ingredients:

1. **One dominant focal subject:** one person, object, place, event, or visual result. It should be identifiable at a glance.
2. **One strong contrast or tension:** before/after, expected/actual, small/large, hidden/revealed, success/failure, two competing possibilities, or another contrast genuinely present in the story.
3. **One proof or stakes cue:** a real detail that makes the idea concrete—an object, visible result, relevant number, map marker, evidence item, or meaningful environmental clue.
4. **One curiosity gap:** leave one understandable question for the title and video to answer. Do not make the image confusing or promise a reveal the video cannot deliver.

**Text rule:** Start with no text. Add a short label only when it makes the idea clearer. Avoid repeating the title; the title and image should contribute different pieces of information. There is no mandatory word count.

**Composition rule:** Use a simple hierarchy, strong subject/background separation, deliberate negative space, and a readable focal point. Rule of thirds is an option, not a law. Faces and expressions are optional; use them only when authentic, relevant, and helpful. Do not force a face into object-led or documentary topics.

## 2. Title-thumbnail partnership

Before designing, write:
- Viewer promise: what the video truly delivers.
- Title angle: what the title explains or asks.
- Thumbnail angle: what visual evidence, tension, or consequence the image adds.
- Factual boundary: what the image must not imply.

The pair must be understandable together, accurate to the finished story, and specific to the intended audience. Do not use a misleading image, fabricated reaction, false scale, altered evidence, fake quote, or unrelated dramatic scene.

## 3. Three concept routes

Generate three genuinely different concepts when the topic supports them—not three cosmetic variations:

- **Object / evidence-led:** the central object or evidence is the hero; best when the subject itself is visually distinctive.
- **Contrast / transformation-led:** two states or outcomes create the tension; best when the story contains a real change or comparison.
- **Human / consequence-led:** an authentic person, decision, or consequence carries the emotion; best when a real human story is central and permissions are clear.

If one route clearly fits the story better, explain why. Do not force all three when the source material cannot support them.

## 4. Production sequence

1. Read the approved brief, research notes, script, and title promise.
2. Extract the strongest truthful visual moment, contrast, proof cue, and unresolved question.
3. Draft three concept routes and pair each with a provisional title.
4. Score each concept qualitatively on:
   - Promise accuracy
   - One-second comprehension
   - Focal clarity
   - Distinctiveness for the intended audience
   - Evidence / proof
   - Small-screen readability
   - Rights, likeness, and attribution
   - Fit with the channel's approved visual identity
5. Recommend one route and state its strongest weakness or counterargument.
6. **Human checkpoint:** show the concepts, matched titles, rationale, and any visual identity change. The user approves the chosen concept before final thumbnail production.
7. Build the thumbnail from owned, licensed, or otherwise authorized assets. Record source, permission, attribution, and any likeness approval.
8. Inspect the actual rendered image at full size and at small mobile-feed size. Check text, subject identity, object details, numbers, labels, logos, cropping, contrast, and factual implications.
9. **Human checkpoint:** present the actual thumbnail image for verification. Do not treat an AI score or internal QA as a substitute for the user's review.
10. Revise only the issues identified; re-inspect the changed image.
11. **Human checkpoint:** user approves the final thumbnail and its title pairing before upload or publication.
12. After publication, review available impressions and CTR by traffic source alongside watch-time/retention and audience fit. Do not judge a thumbnail from CTR alone or from tiny samples.

## 5. Human verification rules

Human review is required at each major creative handoff:
- **Brief → concepts:** verify audience, video promise, and factual boundaries.
- **Concepts → chosen direction:** approve the selected visual idea and title pairing.
- **Generated image → final asset:** inspect the actual pixels and verify factual accuracy, rights, likeness, readability, and visual identity.
- **Final asset → upload:** approve the exact image and title pairing.
- **Post-publish learning:** review the evidence before changing the reusable formula.

Routine reversible improvements—spacing, alignment, contrast, typo fixes, safe crop adjustments—may be made without asking each time, but they must be logged and rechecked. Any change to the core promise, subject, factual meaning, recognizable likeness, overall visual identity, paid generation, or publication requires approval.

If the user is unavailable, save a clearly marked draft and stop at the relevant approval gate. Silence is not approval.

## 6. Technical delivery

- Long-form default: landscape 16:9.
- Use the current YouTube Help upload specifications at delivery time; do not hard-code stale file-size limits.
- Keep important text and focal details away from edges and likely crop zones.
- Export a clean high-resolution image in a supported format.
- Keep editable/source assets when the tool supports them.
- Store final image, prompt/creative brief, asset provenance, review notes, and approval status with the video's project files.

## 7. Scoring and tools

- A model or vidIQ score is directional feedback, not a prediction of CTR or a verdict.
- If using a thumbnail scorer, only score a real image with a valid, relevant title/video context. Do not invent a video ID or use an unrelated proxy.
- For unpublished videos without a valid scoring context, use the qualitative rubric and label separate scoring unavailable.
- Inspect the actual image. A URL, generated score, or textual description does not prove visual quality.
- If image inspection or a renderer is unavailable, mark the thumbnail **unverified draft** rather than passed.
- Do not spend paid credits or publish/upload without explicit user approval.

## 8. Reusable review checklist

- [ ] One clear focal subject
- [ ] One understandable contrast or tension
- [ ] A real proof or stakes cue
- [ ] Title and thumbnail complement rather than duplicate each other
- [ ] The promise is fulfilled by the actual video
- [ ] Accurate subject, context, scale, numbers, labels, and attribution
- [ ] Readable at small size
- [ ] Simple hierarchy; no decorative clutter
- [ ] Rights and likeness permissions checked
- [ ] Fits approved channel identity
- [ ] Actual rendered image inspected
- [ ] Human concept approval recorded
- [ ] Human final-image approval recorded
- [ ] Exact approved image/title pairing recorded before upload
